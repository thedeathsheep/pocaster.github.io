# Editorial and Product Style

## Visual direction

- Editorial, typographic, and research-led; closer to an independent publication than a product-marketing template.
- Strong serif display type may carry identity. Supporting typography stays restrained and legible.
- Use asymmetry, scale, spacing, rules, indices, and metadata deliberately—not as decoration.
- Prefer evidence diagrams, annotated flows, and purposeful visual studies over arbitrary interface screenshots.
- Avoid generic SaaS cards, excessive pills, decorative gradients, dashboard grids, and repeated oversized page titles.
- Every visual element should clarify hierarchy, evidence, system structure, or navigation.

## Case-study structure

Use the dossier sequence when the evidence supports it:

`Situation → Evidence → Tension → Decisions → Prototype → Validation → Archive`

Do not force every case into identical sections. Lead with the product question and the consequential decisions.

## Language

- Site interface and non-article framing copy are English.
- Existing Chinese articles remain Chinese unless translation is explicitly requested.
- Write concise, specific claims. Avoid inflated portfolio language and unexplained metrics.
- Separate observed evidence, interpretation, product decision, and result.
- Do not expose confidential source material merely to make a case appear more complete.

## Change discipline

- Preserve intentional typography and page rhythm across desktop and mobile.
- Check line length, wrapping, dates, navigation, and responsive overflow after layout changes.
- Reuse existing tokens and components before adding another visual system.
